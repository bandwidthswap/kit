# Notes: where the paper is ambiguous and what the simulator does

Against whitepaper v0.5 (Oct 17, 2026), the example installations and the field note *Follow the Bytes, Follow the Money*. Each item names the section, the ambiguity, the simulator's choice and the parameter that changes it where there is one. The choice is always the paper's default where the paper has one, the field note's where the paper has none, and the smaller reading where neither says.

## Section 3

1. **3.3 / 7 / 5.1, the fee on the Steward's own payouts.** 5.1 says the Steward's fraction is taken from every fee paid out of a commitment, the Steward's own included. Implemented: every payout to a relay from any escrow pays *f* to the Mint, which remits it to the Steward. A proving reimbursement to a payer (5.1) and a shortfall refund to a poster are not fees to a relay, so nothing is taken on them.
2. **3.5 / A.3, whose light time.** A.3 widens the tolerance "by the link's light time both ways". The relay checks the payer's timestamp, and the payer may be several links away (an Earth institution paying for a hop on the Moon's surface). Implemented: the base tolerance of the link, plus twice the light time along the path between the payer and the hop, plus the drift rate (`drift_s_per_day`, B9's 0.76 s) times the time since the link's last contact. On the bench that is 5 s and a hair; on the Moon's surface hop it is 5 + 2.56 s.
3. **3.6, a declined receipt.** "A hop that declines a receipt loses the window: its next acknowledgement covers both windows, and the payer's count governs." Implemented as the lab saw it: the declined window is lost and nothing is paid for it; the next window settles on its own. The bytes of the declined window are not carried into the next receipt.
4. **3.6 / 5.1, the unused reservation.** 5.1 says the posted price on a committed window is the poster's to lose, and in the next sentence that an unused reservation pays the carry and nothing else; the field note follows the second. Implemented: a window with nothing sent makes no receipt and pays nothing; its escrow waits for the deadline. Carry is paid only on windows that carried bytes, as the holding time at a relay times the bytes held (`carry` per GB-hour on the link). On the pilot's links the carry is zero, so the two sentences agree there.
5. **3.6 / A.4, the lock and the prepayment.** Neither is modelled: every hop is paid at the window it settles, from the payer of record. A prepaid window whose contact never opens therefore has nothing to refund. A bundle still in custody when the run ends is reported, not paid.
6. **3.4 / A.6, how a payer ranks routes.** The paper ranks by what previous payers settled; a path nobody used ranks nowhere. Implemented: cheapest posted price plus carry, in the payer's unit through the bridges' rates, then shortest delay, then name. A path whose next contact is more than `max_wait` windows away is not taken. Quality is priced only through capacity: `rate_mbps` caps what a hop forwards, and the smaller count pays for what arrived.

## Section 4

7. **2 / 4, the Mint's share.** "Mints are paid from a share of the settlement fee" with no number, and the field note's books show none. Implemented as `mint_share`, default 0, a fraction of each payment kept by the Mint on top of *f*.
8. **4, the ledger and the commitment.** The Merkle commitment is modelled as the list of settlements a Mint published; a bridge relay carries a copy to the Mint on the other side (`mirror_domains`). Inclusion proofs are not modelled, and reconciliation checks only the remittance (item 21).

## Section 5

9. **5, dividing a purchase by opportunity cost.** "A relay with five acceptable alternatives receives one fifth; a single relay connecting a bridge receives all of it." Two sole relays on one path cannot both receive all of it. Implemented: each relay on the path weighs 1 / (1 + the number of acceptable paths that avoid it), normalised over the path's relays; two sole relays split a premium evenly, which is the field note's 6¢ + 6¢. A relay that owns several links on the path takes its share once, spread over its hops.
10. **5.1, the full premium above the minimum.** "At or above the minimum the relay is paid the posted price and the full premium." The field note pays the premium on the bytes moved (12¢ on 1.2 GB at ten cents), not on the minimum (10¢), and the simulator follows it. Below the minimum the premium is paid in proportion and the rest of that window's premium returns at once, as 5.1 says. Consequence: an escrow sized at the minimum (720 × 1 GB × $0.25 = $180) is drawn at 30¢ a full window and runs out before 720 windows. The paper should say which it means.
11. **5.1, an escrow that runs out.** Not stated. Implemented: the escrow pays what it has toward the posted price, is marked exhausted, and the poster pays the rest of that window and every later window directly, with no premium.
12. **5.1, the shortfall per window.** The rate is "the receipt's byte count over the payer's first and last timestamps". Implemented per bundle at delivery: premium × (minimum − delivered bytes) returns when a bundle delivers less than the minimum. A poster with several bundles in one window would be refunded on each; the shipped scenarios have one.
13. **5.1, the proving allowance.** Reimbursed windows are counted per period (`proving_period`, 168 windows) and do not count toward the commitment's windows; the reimbursement is the posted price the tester paid, fee included, as the field note's 4.5¢ back shows. Eligibility is a bond older than the commitment and, when `sees_ownership` is on, a payer not owned by any relay on the path.
14. **5.1 / 10, ownership, and the finding that changed the rule.** 5.1 excludes payers "owned by the relay" from proving and Section 5 from the Steward's purchases; Section 10 says the protocol cannot see ownership. Implemented as `sees_ownership` (default true in scenario files, 5.1 as written; the attacks run with it off, Section 10's reading); every node carries an `owner` the simulator can see. **Finding (first version of this simulator):** with ownership unseen, a Steward pool holding other people's fees, and the second-bridge bounty sized at the sole link's whole fees, a wash trader whose own link is a sole bridge built a second anchor on a second mapped place it owned, moved its traffic there, collected the bounty on it and netted about +8¢ a dollar at the paper's defaults: Section 3.3's condition ("no payer may get back through the Steward as much as the Steward took from it") held for the match and failed for program 2. **The rule now implemented, which the paper will adopt:** (a) a second-bridge bounty is sized from the Steward's income on the sole link, *f* times the fees it carried (item 16), and (b) a per-payer cap at every Steward payout, across all three programs (item 35). With both, the trader nets −2.62¢ a dollar at the defaults by the match alone, −2.11¢ at best when it also builds and serves the second path, and no *m* up to 3*f* turns it positive; splitting its traffic across 1, 2 or 4 payer NYMs gives the same figure to the cent, since the caps add up to one bound.
15. **5.1, pipes and places.** A pipe (no end date) is a commitment with a deadline past the run. Access points, anchors' control-plane exchanges and per-payer caps at a place are not modelled; a place is a node's `place`, and a path is through a place when a node on it is there.

## Section 7

16. **7, sizing the second bridge.** "Sized from the fees the sole link carried over the program's window." The first version of this simulator read that as the gross fees, which is what let a wash trader take back more than *f* (item 14). Implemented now as the Steward's income from the link: value = *f* times the gross payments to the link's owner for carriage on it during the program window, capped by the escrow available (item 19); premium per GB = that value over the bytes settled, which is *f* of the link's effective price, so the Steward's take of a toll is the bounty for the next bridge; minimum rate = the link's bytes per window; windows = the program window; deadline = `bounty_deadline` windows after posting (the paper gives the Steward's commitments none). Unspent escrow returns at the deadline and the link is considered again at the next program window, so a bounty nobody serves is reposted at the current fees. On the street with House 1's dish down that is 3¢ a day, 5% of the Wi-Fi's 60¢.
17. **7, "between its ends".** Read literally, every priced link into a relay is a sole bridge (nothing else reaches that relay), and a monopolist's second key on the same hill is a second link rather than the same bridge. Implemented: the ends are the ends of the traffic the link carried in the program window, the nodes it came from and went to, and the existing path's places are those of the paths that traffic took. The link is sole when the map has no path from an origin to a destination that avoids it and passes through a mapped place not on those paths. On the street with House 1's dish down this makes the Wi-Fi the one sole bridge and House 3's hill the place that counts, as the field note says; on the valley it makes two keys on one hill one bridge and a key on another hill a second.
18. **7, the match.** "The Steward adds to it, up to a fraction *m* of the payer's escrow" and "the matching program spends at most *m* of the Steward's income." Both caps are applied: a match is the smaller of *m* × the escrow, *m* × the Steward's income to date less matches made, and the escrow in hand. Income includes `steward.opening_escrow`, the fees taken before the run. A match "adds to" the commitment by buying more windows at the poster's own terms (the matched amount over the poster's value per window, rounded down), so a fully served commitment collects its escrow plus the match, as Section 3.3 describes. The match is made when the commitment is posted and kept apart from the poster's money: the poster's escrow is drawn first, the Steward's after it, and every draw of the Steward's money is a Steward payout under the cap of item 35. Payers whose bond is younger than `min_bond_age` windows get none.
19. **7, "second bridges first, first links from what is left".** Implemented: the unspent match allowance (*m* × income − matched) is held in reserve; the second-bridge program spends from the rest, largest fees first; the first-link program spends what remains. A bounty the budget cannot fund is logged and skipped.
20. **7, the first link.** "Every mapped place with no link gets a standing commitment ... renewed until a receipt settles across it." Implemented: a place in the domain, mapped and in the map, with no settled receipt on any link at it, gets a commitment of `first_link_size` (default 0, off) at `first_link_premium` per GB, open for `bounty_deadline` windows, paid as a premium on eligible payers' bytes across any link at the place, and reposted while no receipt has settled there. The map's acceptance of a place is a declaration (`places[].since`, or a `map_place` event), not a reconciliation.
21. **7 Execution / A.5, auditing the Steward's postings.** Not modelled. Reconciliation audits only each Mint's remittance (fee due against fee remitted on its published settlements since the Mints last met).
22. **7, refunds.** Escrow a commitment returns at its deadline or completion goes back to whoever put it in: the poster's money to the poster, the Steward's (a match, or a whole bounty) to the Steward's escrow; a shortfall refund (item 12) is drawn from the poster's money alone. The cabin's $4.50 match therefore comes back whole when the ridge goes dark before the matched windows are reached.

## Appendix A

23. **A.1, parameters carried but not acted on.** The signature scheme and receipt lifetime are recorded on every link and never checked. Light time does not delay a bundle (a contact is a window; the light time is inside it) and enters only the clock tolerance. A contact plan is periodic (`every`, `duration`, `offset`); real plans come from ephemerides.
24. **A.2, custody.** A relay with `store_and_forward` forwards a bundle at the earliest open contact of its next link strictly after the window it received it, and is paid carry for the windows held. The custody receipt is the countersignature on take-up; loss of a bundle in custody is not modelled, so nothing is slashed for it.
25. **A.5, the bridge's price "embeds the rate".** Implemented as separate rows rather than one price: the payer pays each hop's posted price in that link's unit, and a bridge bills what it paid on the far side back toward the payer at its own rate (`rates` on the bridge node, `passes_upstream` on its near-side link). The field note's $63 is $20 + $3 carry + $40 converted. With `passes_upstream` off, the bridge's own price covers the far hops, which is how the Jupiter orbiter pays the Europa lander "its share" out of its $50.
26. **A.5, reconciliation as traffic and as a deposit.** Reconciliation is free and instantaneous in the simulator when any chain of open links joins the two domains at a reconciliation window (`reconcile_every`); the deposit of each other's commitment is logged, not stored; a bridge that withholds reconciliation is not modelled.
27. **A.5, exclusion.** A Mint whose published settlements show less remitted than *f* requires since the last reconciliation is excluded by the Mint that finds it, which stops reconciling with it. A relay bonded on both sides then either takes down its links that settle in the excluded domain (`bridge_after_exclusion = "stop"`, the default) or keeps settling and forfeits its bond on the honest side at the next window it does (`"keep"`). Readmission is not modelled.
28. **A.5, the map.** Domains and places are declared in the scenario; Mint consensus on them is not simulated.

35. **3.3 made a rule: the per-payer payout cap.** Section 3.3's condition, "no payer may get back through the Steward as much as the Steward took from it", is stated for the match (*m* strictly below *f*) and nowhere enforced for programs 2 and 3. Implemented as a rule at every Steward payout, across all three programs: on receipts whose payer is a given NYM, the Steward never pays out more than (*f* − `steward_margin`, default 0.5%) of the fees that NYM has paid in for carriage in the domain, counted per payer NYM with no view of ownership, since every Steward payout is against a receipt and every receipt names its payer. "Paid in" is the gross posted price and carry on the payer's settled receipts, whether the payer paid it directly or from its own escrow; premiums are not fees in. A payout the cap holds back stays in the commitment's pool and returns to the Steward at its deadline; the flow table reports what the cap held. The cap applies to the Steward's money only: a poster's own escrow pays its relays whatever it bought. Consequence for the match: a match above *f* − margin can be posted but not collected, so the sweep's curve is flat beyond it. Consequence for the second bridge: a bounty collected on a monopolist's own customers' receipts (the valley) is well inside the cap, since those payers paid the toll for weeks.

36. **7, a link inside one place (added Oct 18, 2026 with the kit's Steward).** The second-bridge program wanted to post a bounty for a second path around the lab's bench wire, which runs from the house to the house. There is no second place to go through, so there is nothing to post for: a link whose traffic's origin and destination places are one place is skipped and logged. The simulator's scenarios never met this because each gave the two ends of every link different places (the bench scenario puts bw-forest at "the forest"); the lab's map does not.
37. **7, the first link's top-up (added Oct 18, 2026 with the kit's Steward).** A standing offer "sized by a parameter" that the budget could only part-fund was posted once at what the budget allowed and left there until its deadline, so on a lab whose Steward earns a hundredth of a cent an hour it would sit at a hundredth of a cent for a week. Now, while the offer stands, each run tops it up toward `first_link_size` from what the budget allows (`steward_rules.first_link_size`), as an addition to the same commitment. The cabin's Steward has $200 and funds its offer whole, so no shipped scenario's numbers change.
38. **One rule in one file.** The cap, the draw, the match, the budget, the two bounty sizes and the premium split are `steward_rules.py` at the kit's root, imported by this simulator and by the Mint's Steward in `bwkit.py`, so the two cannot drift; `sim/tests.py` and `test_bwkit.py` both hold the functions to the field note's figures.

## The installations and the field note

29. **The bench and the fee.** The running kit remits nothing (there is no Steward yet); the bench scenario applies v0.5's 5% so its books close as the paper's do.
30. **The street's own traffic.** The field note gives House 1 twenty gigabytes a day of its own; Houses 2 and 3 are given none, since their own use over their own dishes pays nobody.
31. **The cabin's escrow and the Steward's income.** The match is bounded by *m* of the Steward's income (item 18); for the field note's "a little larger" to show, the street's Steward is given $200 of fee income from before the run (`steward.opening_escrow`). Without it the match is 2.5% of whatever the Steward has, which on a street earning 35¢ a day is pennies.
32. **Earth to Moon in one unit.** Two bookkeepers, one unit, as the installations ask; the fee goes to the Steward of the domain the link settles in (Earth, the payer's), and the Moon Mint holds the relay's copy.
33. **The Jupiter orbiter's price.** Earth pays $50 and the orbiter keeps $47.50, as the field note draws; the Europa lander is paid 2 JUP a gigabyte by the orbiter in Jupiter's unit out of that price (`passes_upstream = false`), a number the field note leaves as "its share".
34. **Six-hour contacts.** The planetary scenario makes the window a six-hour contact so that one window of custody at Mars is the field note's six hours and $3 of carry; the conjunction is 84 windows (three weeks).
